02 · The Team
Four roles. One team.
Direction

Product Manager

  • Defines problem and success
  • Chooses the wedge use case
  • Runs priorities and customer learning
  • Owns decision rhythm and narrative
Architecture

Solution Architect

  • Shapes the end-to-end system concept
  • Reduces technical risk early
  • Finds what can be reused vs. invented
  • Translates the demo into scalable architecture
Experience

Front-End Dev

  • Builds visible user experience fast
  • Makes the demo feel real and intuitive
  • Turns concepts into clickable proof
  • Captures workflow friction quickly
Logic

Back-End Dev

  • Builds core logic and integrations
  • Creates realistic data, APIs, rules
  • Makes workflow outcomes measurable
  • Stress-tests feasibility early

04 · Plugging Into the Organization
Five steps into the organization.
1

Find the
wedge

One high-pain, high-visibility workflow — frustration is obvious, success is easy to observe.

2

Define the
hypothesis

"If we show this outcome clearly, the customer will care, lean in, or ask for a next step."

3

Build a
thin-slice POC

Only the minimum workflow, data logic, and UI needed to prove customer value.

4

Demo
with intent

Test appetite, objections, trust, and operational fit — not just to impress.

5

11 · Leadership Style
Learn from three leaders.

Steve Jobs

  • Obsess over user experience
  • Make the story simple
  • Say no to noise
  • Demo the future, not the features

Jensen Huang

  • Think full-stack, not in silos
  • Master the details that change outcomes
  • Tell a clear platform narrative
  • Relentlessly compound capability

Elon Musk

  • Reason from first principles
  • Remove unnecessary steps
  • Iterate faster than competitors think
  • Push for useful truth from real tests
