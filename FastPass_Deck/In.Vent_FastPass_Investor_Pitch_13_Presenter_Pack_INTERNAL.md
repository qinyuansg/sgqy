## 5. Q&A bank (42 questions)


### Strategy

**Q: Is this a venture-scale business, or a good product line for ST Engineering?**  
We can't prove venture scale today. The US$42–94M a year beachhead is deliberately narrow: automated border control e-gates and kiosks in two regions. The path to US$100M+ is a hypothesis: every departure touchpoint plus platform software, then congested airports worldwide, then seaports and land borders. The pilot has to open that path. If it doesn't, FastPass is a solid Group product line, not a venture, and the milestone structure tells us early.

**Q: Why now, and not three years ago or three years from now?**  
Three things moved in 2024–26. 54% of surveyed airports already run biometric border control, with 83% expected by 2028 (SITA). 79% of surveyed passengers say they're ready to carry their passport on their phone (SITA). And ICAO proposed standards for phone-held travel credentials in April 2026, though they are not yet adopted. Our first pilot targets 2027, inside that window. The risk on the other side is that the replacement cycle is being won now; that is why gate retrofit matters [CTO to confirm].

**Q: What happens if border agencies don't value a second factor?**  
Then our wedge is weaker, and we say so. The lane would compete as one more face-only corridor, against IDEMIA, NEC and Amadeus. ### Security & privacy

**Q: How is a Bluetooth signal a second factor? What stops a relay attack or the wrong phone being paired in a crowd?**  
The design intent is that the lane cryptographically challenges the credential on the paired phone, and the live face must match that credential [CTO to confirm]. A wrong pairing should then produce a no-match exception routed to an officer, not a false accept. That protects security but can cost throughput in crowds. Independent ISO/IEC 30107-3 and 19795 tests, plus BLE relay and mis-pairing tests, are M2's exit criteria. We make no claim on these before M2.

**Q: How do you handle spoofing and demographic bias in face matching?**  
Presentation-attack testing to ISO/IEC 30107-3, biometric performance testing to ISO/IEC 19795 and demographic accuracy testing are all in the plan [status]. The false-accept and false-reject rates are go/no-go measures on the pilot scorecard. We have no independent results yet.

**Q: Where does traveller data live, who controls it and how long is it kept?**  
The credential is encrypted and stays on the traveller's phone. Enrolment is opt-in, and documents are accepted at any point. The data controller and retention model are set per deployment and country, under laws such as Singapore's PDPA, the UAE and Saudi PDPLs, Indonesia's PDP Law and GDPR for EU travellers [legal review]. IATA's 2025 survey found 42% of travellers hesitant about biometrics would reconsider if data protection were guaranteed.

**Q: Your earlier screens showed names, faces and ID numbers. Are those real travellers?**  
Every screen in this deck was rebuilt with synthetic data: masked IDs, silhouettes, sequence numbers. Public displays show no personal data, just a sequence number and CLEARED. Say this only once the rebuild is actually done, and confirm in writing whether the original images were AI-generated.

**Q: What is the cyber exposure of putting identity credentials on phones and a BLE link in the terminal?**  
Spoofed beacons, relay attacks, tailgating, compromised phones and platform intrusion are all in the A8 threat model [mitigations to document]. ST Engineering's cybersecurity practice is an asset here, but we won't claim any certification until it exists.
